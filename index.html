About

I'm an AI/ML engineer finishing my M.S. in Artificial Intelligence at Northwestern. At the PedzSTAR Lab I work on pediatric speech — extracting a child's voice out of noisy three-microphone clinical recordings, and fine-tuning speech models to screen for speech pathology. Before Northwestern I spent two years as a software and ML engineer in Chennai, going from ML Intern to Associate Software Engineer and shipping document-AI and computer-vision systems into production for logistics, aviation and civic clients.

I've also worked at Northwestern's Global Poverty Research Lab, on Devidence — an AI pipeline that pulls key results out of development-economics papers using GPT-4o and table detection, benchmarked against 12,000+ expert-entered records from 53 studies. My main contribution there was diagnostic: I found the pipeline's real bottleneck was locating the right numbers at all (387 rows returned where only 143 were real) and built a stricter evaluation showing true performance was about half the original estimate.

What I like most is the unglamorous middle of the problem: the evaluation that shows a pipeline is half as good as everyone thought, the cross-microphone trick that removes an adult's voice without touching the child's, the SLURM job that makes 220 hours of audio re-annotatable without moving a byte. I care about building models that hold up outside the benchmark, and about testing them honestly.

Outside the lab I've spent a lot of time building teams rather than models — as General Secretary of VIT's Tech Researchers Club, on the Girl Up chapter's outreach team, and, during India's second COVID wave, coordinating 250+ volunteers who connected around 5,000 people with medical supplies, hospital beds and oxygen.

Child speech extraction & speaker diarization — 3-mic clinical audio

  • Raised adult-speech removal from 47.5% to 72.7% at 97% child-speech recall by fusing a 10 ms Whisper-based child speech detector with 56 cross-microphone crosstalk features.
  • Suppressed adult-only speech by 37–95 dB with 0.00 dB median change to child audio, using non-generative voice isolation via cross-mic leakage unmixing.
  • Deployed across 220 hours of audio (1,529 sessions, 305 children) on SLURM with a lossless archive and versioned confidence tracks, enabling full-corpus re-annotation without data re-transfer.

Speech Intelligibility Prediction (SIP) — pediatric intelligibility screening

  • Developing SIP, a research framework that separates intelligibility measurement from ASR confidence — standard confidence reflects how decisively a model chose an output, while SIP measures how much the acoustic signal itself constrained the space of possible interpretations, a more clinically meaningful quantity for developmental and atypical speech, where WER is unreliable.
  • Flagged high-risk speech pathology at 69.4% accuracy by LoRA fine-tuning 16 ASR models (Whisper, Wav2Vec2, HuBERT) and deriving uncertainty metrics correlated with clinical scores.
  • Scaled inference to 131,000+ child utterances with batched GPU pipelines on HPC.
